This "real-deal" Kung Pao Chicken from J. Kenji López-Alt is modeled after the flavors of Chengdu, prioritizing a nuanced balance of "ma-la" (numbing and hot). By infusing the oil with Sichuan peppercorns and dried chiles, you create a signature aromatic base for the tender, marinated chicken.

---

## Gong Bao Ji Ding (Sichuan Kung Pao Chicken)

**By J. Kenji López-Alt**

- **Yield:** Serves 2
    
- **Active Time:** 15 minutes
    
- **Total Time:** 15 minutes
    

### Ingredients

**For the Chicken**

- **2 small** boneless skinless chicken breasts (about 12 oz total), cut into 1/2-inch cubes
    
- **1 tsp** Shaoxing wine (or dry sherry)
    
- **2 tsp** light soy sauce
    
- **2 tsp** cornstarch
    
- **Large pinch** kosher salt
    

**For the Sauce**

- **1 tbsp** honey
    
- **2 tbsp** Chinkiang vinegar (or balsamic/Chinese black vinegar)
    
- **1 tbsp** Shaoxing wine
    
- **2 tsp** light soy sauce
    
- **1/2 tsp** cornstarch
    
- **As needed:** Water or low-sodium chicken stock
    

**For the Stir-Fry**

- **3 tbsp** vegetable oil
    
- **6 to 12** small dried red chiles (e.g., árbol), stems removed, cut into 1/2-inch pieces, seeds discarded
    
- **1 tsp** Sichuan peppercorn husks (stems and black seeds discarded)
    
- **4 medium** cloves garlic, thinly sliced
    
- **1-inch knob** ginger, peeled and cut into fine matchsticks or grated
    
- **6** scallions, white and pale green parts only, cut into 1/2-inch pieces
    
- **3/4 cup** roasted peanuts
    

---

### Directions

#### 1. Marinate the Chicken

In a small bowl, combine the chicken cubes, wine, soy sauce, cornstarch, and salt. Stir until the chicken is evenly coated in a thin film of cornstarch paste and set aside.

#### 2. Prepare the Sauce

Whisk the honey, vinegar, wine, soy sauce, and cornstarch together in a small bowl. Stir with a fork until the cornstarch is completely dissolved and no clumps remain.

#### 3. Infuse the Oil and Sear

Rub a small amount of oil into a large wok or skillet and preheat over high heat until smoking. Add the remaining oil, then immediately add the dried chiles and Sichuan peppercorns. Stir-fry for about **5 seconds** until fragrant but not burnt. Immediately add the chicken and stir-fry for **45 to 90 seconds** until the pink spots disappear from the exterior.

#### 4. Aromatics and Peanuts

Add the garlic and ginger to the wok and stir-fry for **10 seconds**. Toss in the scallions and peanuts and continue stir-frying for another **30 seconds**.

#### 5. Finish and Serve

Pour in the sauce ingredients. Stir-fry for about **1 minute** until the chicken is cooked through and everything is evenly coated in a glossy glaze. If the sauce becomes too thick or clumps, add water **1 tablespoon at a time** to loosen it. Serve immediately with steamed white rice.

---

### Pro-Tips & Notes

- **Light Soy Sauce:** Do not confuse this with "low-sodium" soy sauce. Light soy is thinner and saltier than dark soy; if unavailable, use Japanese shoyu or tamari.
    
- **Wok Heat:** This recipe is designed for two. Doubling it in a single batch can crowd the pan, causing the temperature to drop and preventing a proper sear. If cooking for four, prepare the chicken and vegetables in two separate batches and combine them at the very end when adding the sauce.
    
- **Prep is Key:** Because the stir-fry happens in under 4 minutes, ensure all ingredients are chopped and sauce is mixed before turning on the heat.
