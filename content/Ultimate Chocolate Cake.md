# In Need of The Ultimate Chocolate Cake Recipe - Ask the Chef

My own quest for the perfect chocolate cake took the better part of a decade. I wanted to get volume, structure, moistness and tenderness in the same cake, and countless recipes, some from extremely pricy trade publications, failed time and again.

Most recipes called for melting semi-sweet chocolate and pouring it into the batter, and while this was surely a "chocolate cake" it was never quite chocolaty enough to suit my taste. I needed a cake so rich in chocolate flavor that one would think chocolate is about to ooze through the pores of his skin.

Adding more chocolate simply made the cake suffer in volume and tenderness. Nothing I tried or conceived of worked worth a damn. Finally, I found a dusty old recipe in The Settlement Cookbook , from the year 1901. Unlike any recipe I had previously seen, the author made a kind of pudding with chocolate, water and sugar. Adding hot water to cocoa solids causes the cocoa to bloom, enhancing the chocolate flavor in the finished cake.

I learned that, when unsweetened chocolate and cocoa powder are combined with hot water they form an emulsion- tiny droplets of cocoa solids and cocoa butter disperse, causing a noticeably more chocolaty cake. When sugar is also added the effect was even more dramatic. Why? Sugar has a strong affinity to water. Sugar molecules bond tightly with water molecules, leaving the cocoa solids free to dissolve in the cocoa butter; fat being a better medium for transferring flavor than water (see my earlier post on "Fat as the Sixth Taste Receptor" ).

CAKE

12 Tablespoons (1 1/2 sticks) unsalted butter, very soft, plus extra for greasing pans.

1 3/4 cups (8 3/4 oz) unbleached All Purpose flour, plus extra for dusting pans.

4 ounces unsweetened chocolate, coarsely chopped

1/4 cup (3/4 oz) Dutch Processed Cocoa Powder

1/2 cup hot water

1 3/4 cup (12 1/4 oz) sugar

1 1/2 teaspoons baking soda

1 teaspoon table salt

1 cup buttermilk

2 teaspoon vanilla extract

4 large eggs, plus 2 egg yolks

FROSTING

16 ounces semi-sweet chocolate, finely chopped

8 Tablespoons (1 stick) unsalted butter

1/3 cup sugar

2 Tablespoons corn syrup

2 teaspoons vanilla extract

1/4 teaspoon table salt

1 1/4 cups cold heavy cream

METHOD

Adjust oven rack to middle; heat to 350 degrees. Grease 2 9-inch by 2-inch cake pans with softened butter and flour, dumping out excess. Combine chocolate, cocoa powder and hot water in a medium heat-proof bowl; set bowl over saucepan containing 1-inch of simmering water and stir with a rubber spatula until the chocolate is melted, about 2-minutes. Add 1/2 cup sugar to the chocolate mixture and stir until thick and glossy, about 2 minutes. Remove bowl from heat and set aside to cool.

Whisk flour, baking soda and salt in a medium bowl. Combine buttermilk and vanilla in a small bowl. In the bowl of a standing mixer fitted with a whisk attachment, whisk eggs and yolks on medium-low speed until combined, about 10-seconds. Add remaining 1 1/4 cups of sugar, increase spped to high and whisk until fluffy and lightened in color, about 3 minutes. Replace whisk with the paddle attachment. Add cooled chocolate mixture to the egg/sugar mixture and mix on medium speed until thoroughly incorporated, about 45-seconds, pushing a spatula to scrape down the sides of the bowl as needed. Add softened butter, 1 tablespoon at a time, mixing about 10-seconds after each addition. Add about 1/3rd of the flour mixture, followed by half of the buttermilk mixture, mixing until incorporated after each addition (about 15-seconds). Repeat using half of the remaining flour mixture and all the remaining buttermilk mixture (batter may appear seperated). Scrape down sides of bowl and add remaining flour mixture, mix at medium-low speed until batter is thoroughly combined, about 15-seconds. Remove bowl from the mixer and fold batter once or twice with the spatula to incorporate any remaining flour. Divide into cake pans and smooth to the edge with a spatula.

Bake cakes until toothpick comes out clean, about 25-30 minutes. Cool cakes for 15-minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack to cool to room temperature, about 1-hour.

TO MAKE FROSTING

Melt chocolate in bowl set over simmering water, stirring until smooth. Remove from heat and set aside. Meanwhile, heat butter in a small saucepan over medium-low heat until melted. Add sugar, corn syrup, vanilla and salt, increase heat to medium and stir with a spatula until dissolved, about 4-minutes. Add melted cocolate, butter mixture and cream to the clean bowl of a mixer and stir thoroughly to combine.

Place the bowl over an ice bath and stir with a spatula until it begins to thicken and stick to the sides of the bowl (about 2-minutes). It should be about 70-degrees. Place on the mixer with a paddle and beat on medium-high speed until light and fluffy, scraping bowl as needed, about 2-minutes.
