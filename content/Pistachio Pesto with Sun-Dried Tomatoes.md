[[Recipes]]

This is a fantastic twist on a traditional pesto. By swapping pine nuts for pistachios and adding sun-dried cherry tomatoes, you’re moving toward a _Pesto alla Trapanese_ style—rich, slightly sweet, and incredibly vibrant.

Here is how to bring these ingredients together for a perfect consistency.

---

## Sun-Dried Tomato & Pistachio Pesto

### Prep time: 10 minutes | Yields: Approx. 150g

### Instructions

1. **Prep the Pistachios & Garlic:** If your pistachios have skins, you can give them a quick rub in a kitchen towel to remove the excess (though leaving them on adds a nice rustic texture). Finely mince the **½ garlic clove** first so you don't end up with a large spicy chunk in the final sauce.
    
2. **The "Pulse" Phase:** In a food processor (or mortar and pestle for the purists), combine the **pistachios**, **garlic**, and **dried cherry tomatoes**. Pulse until you have a coarse, pebbly paste.
    
3. **Add the Greens & Cheese:** Add the **6 basil leaves** and the **grated Parmigiano Reggiano**.
    
    > **Pro Tip:** Pulse in short bursts. If you over-process basil, the blades can heat up and oxidize the leaves, turning them brown.
    
4. **Emulsify:** While the processor is on low (or while stirring constantly), slowly stream in the **50g of extra virgin olive oil**.
    
5. **Adjust Consistency (The "j.e." Factor):** Your recipe calls for **water j.e.** (_quanto basta_ or "as much as is enough"). Add water one teaspoon at a time until the pesto reaches a creamy, spreadable consistency.
    
6. **Season:** Taste it first! Between the Parmigiano and the dried tomatoes, you may not need much **salt**. Add a pinch only if necessary.
    

---

### Best Ways to Use This

- **Pasta:** Toss with _busiate_ or _fusilli_ (the spirals catch the nutty bits perfectly).
    
- **Bruschetta:** Smear a thick layer on toasted sourdough and top with a slice of fresh mozzarella.
    
- **Proteins:** This works beautifully as a crust for baked sea bass or roasted chicken breast.
    

**Storage Note:** If you aren't using it immediately, put it in a jar and cover the surface with a thin layer of olive oil to keep it fresh. It will stay happy in the fridge for about 3–4 days. Enjoy!
