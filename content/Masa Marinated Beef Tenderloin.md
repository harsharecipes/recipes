## Just Like Abuelita

by CHEF MICHAEL FLORES on MARCH 5, 2010 · 1 COMMENT

When I was little, I used to eat my grandmother’s meals and think I died and went to heaven because they were so good. You know how childhood memories are often exaggerated in your mind? For instance, you remember going fishing and catching a huge tuna when in reality it was probably the size of a goldfish. Well, this was nothing like that. Her cooking was the real deal.

Traditional Mexican cuisine is remembered and characterized by the comfort foods made by abuelitas, the real masters of the kitchen. Back in the days, our grandmothers had to beat the masa and make corn and wheat tortillas by hand. The hours of work in the kitchen and the love that went into cooking meals for their family was instantly recognizable. Everything they did was created organically; no pre-packaged or canned foods, and definitely no shortcuts.

By the time the food reached the 2nd and 3rd generation Latinos who immigrated to America, much of the authenticity had been lost. The mothers never got the recipes because they were told to get out of the kitchen while grandma worked her magic. But she still remembers the smells of the kitchen and the flavors she tasted as a child, which she tries to replicate and convey through her own cooking. As 2nd and 3rd generation Latino-Americans adopt American cultures, the idea of traditional, authentic Mexican food becomes more and more diluted with American influences. Recipes of rich, diverse flavors and textures are lost in the cultural mixing as today’s Mexican food is now written off as too greasy and spicy. This is why it’s important to re-introduce traditional Latin cuisine to the new generation, so we don’t lose that vital essence of our culture.

The “ MASECA en la Cocina con las Estrellas ” program is a cooking segment that airs every Thursdays on the Spanish morning show, Despierta America. The show features various chefs cooking Mexican food using MASECA corn flour products . Corn flour is a healthy ingredient to be used in your dishes and also provides more authentic Latin flavor. The show encourages people to follow along with their favorite stars on TV to cook healthy, traditional meals at home for the entire family. Hopefully, more shows like this will continue to promote abuelita’s legacy to be passed on for generations. Below is a recipe that uses corn masa flour , which will conjure up memories of abuelita’s home-cooked meals.

MASA MARINATED BEEF TENDERLOIN

Maseca Instant Corn Flour


3 pounds beef tenderloin
1½ cups [instant corn masa flour](http://www.mexgrocer.com/2440.html)(i.e., Maseca®)
1½ cups water
1 cup vegetable oil
½ cup fresh squeezed lime juice
½ cup apple cider vinegar
1 white onion, roughly chopped
5 garlic cloves
1 fresh jalapeño
2 tablespoons ground coriander
1 teaspoon saltPut the beef tenderloin in a one gallon zipper bag and set aside. Purée the remaining ingredients in a blender until smooth. Pour the blended mixture over the beef and “squish” around to make sure the meat is totally covered; refrigerate for 24 hours.

To cook, preheat your oven to 425°F. In the mean time, remove the meat from the marinade (shake off any excess marinade) and place in a roasting pan. Season with salt and pepper. Roast in the oven for 25 minutes—or until desired doneness is reached. Allow to rest for 5-7 minutes before serving.

Serves 6-8 people
